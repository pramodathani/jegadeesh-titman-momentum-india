Abstract

We rebuild the 32 relative-strength strategies of Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) on every share traded on India's National Stock Exchange from 2005 to 2026, including shares later delisted, with returns adjusted for splits, bonuses and dividends. Momentum is stronger in India than in the original US sample: among the 500 most-traded shares, buying the top decile and selling the bottom decile earns 1.6% to 1.8% a month with a three-month holding period. Because shares cannot be shorted overnight in India's cash market, we also hold the winners decile alone. After Indian trading costs, the best such portfolio (nine-month ranking, three-month holding) compounded at … a year from 2007 to 2026, against … for the NIFTY 500 with dividends, yet it fell 73% in 2008. This page lets the reader choose any of the 64 portfolios and inspect its returns, its current and historical constituents, its joiners and leavers each month, and its risk.

1. Introduction

Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) showed that US stocks with the highest returns over the past three to twelve months kept outperforming those with the lowest over the following three to twelve months. Their strategy ranks stocks each month on their past J-month return, buys the top decile, sells the bottom decile, and holds each position for K months, so that K overlapping positions are held at once, each with 1/K of the capital. A second set of strategies waits one week between ranking and buying.

This companion applies the same construction to Indian shares. Every figure and table below responds to the portfolio specification above. Throughout, "the portfolio" is the long-only winners decile, equally weighted within each monthly cohort and rebalanced monthly, measured after Indian delivery costs: securities transaction tax, stamp duty, exchange and regulatory charges, and an assumed market impact of 0.1% per side for the 500 most-traded shares and 0.3% for all shares.

8. Data and methods

Share prices come from the National Stock Exchange's daily bhavcopy files. NSE does not adjust the previous close on an ex-date, so returns are adjusted with NSE's own list of splits, bonuses, consolidations and dividends, which is complete from 2005; each split or bonus is placed on the trading day near its listed ex-date whose price change best matches its ratio. Daily returns below −50% or above +100% are treated as data errors. Monthly returns compound daily returns within each calendar month. A share is eligible for ranking if it traded in each of the J + 1 months up to the formation date; the "500 most traded" universe keeps the 500 eligible shares with the highest median daily traded value over the previous six months. The benchmark is the NIFTY 500 price index plus one twelfth of its reported dividend yield each month.

The study's full tables, the code that produces every number on this page, and the caveats are in the repository. The best of 64 backtests is partly luck: with 64 strategies tested, a single one needs a t-statistic near 3.2 to rule out chance. Market impact is assumed rather than measured, and momentum portfolios can lose most of their value in a crash. Nothing here is investment advice.
